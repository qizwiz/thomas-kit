Thomas.

Your own assistant. One brain. One persona you design.

It runs on your machine. Your prompts go to the brain you chose, under your own login.

One line.

curl -fsSL https://qizwiz.github.io/thomas-kit/install | sh

Where to paste it: a terminal, the text window where you type commands. On a Mac press ⌘+Space, type Terminal and press Return; on Linux open the app called Terminal. Click Copy above, paste into that window, and press Return.

It installs Thomas in your home folder, adds one marked line to your shell profile if needed so a new terminal knows the thomas command, then checks that everything it needs is there and tells you how to fix anything missing. Needs Python 3.10+. The installer itself needs no admin rights (on Debian or Ubuntu you may first need sudo apt install python3-venv).

For the Claude brain you need a paid Claude account. The Claude brain runs on Claude Code, which needs a Pro, Max, Team or Enterprise plan, or a Claude Console account, billed by API usage; the free claude.ai plan does not include it. Get one at claude.ai (or platform.claude.com). You do not need to install anything else for the sign-in: the installer brings Claude Code's sign-in along, and thomas login opens it in your browser (on a computer without one, it prints a link to open on your phone and a code to paste back). Or set ANTHROPIC_API_KEY.

No paid account? Start free. Run thomas login --free and paste a free OpenRouter key: sign in at openrouter.ai with Google or GitHub, open openrouter.ai/keys and press Create API Key; no card. Thomas then answers with OpenRouter's free models. The free brain answers in text only (it cannot read your files or run anything), and free models have a daily request limit. They are run by outside companies that may keep, train on, or publish what you send, including your notes and recent conversation, which Thomas sends with every question, so do not send anything private; OpenRouter may ask you to allow this in its privacy settings before free models work. thomas login switches you to Claude later.

Works on macOS and Linux. Windows: run it inside WSL, which looks like Linux to the installer; we have not tested it there yet.

Read the installer before you run it.

Details: what it does, and what the checksum does and does not prove

It downloads the kit, checks its SHA-256 against the published checksum, installs into ~/.local/share/thomas, links ~/.local/bin/thomas if nothing is there, and, only if ~/.local/bin is not already on your PATH, adds export PATH="$HOME/.local/bin:$PATH" once to your shell profile -- ~/.zshrc (zsh), ~/.bashrc (bash on Linux), on macOS bash the first of ~/.bash_profile, ~/.bash_login or ~/.profile that exists, or ~/.profile for other shells -- marked # added by the Thomas installer so you can find and delete it (fish, csh and tcsh users get the command to run instead). Then it runs thomas doctor, which names anything still missing and the exact fix. For the Claude brain it puts the adapter in its own folder, fetching Node 22 there too if you do not have it.

The checksum catches a corrupted download; it does not protect against a compromised host, since both files come from the same place.

Who it is.
Your call.

thomas login           # your login, never ours
thomas persona set :name Ada
thomas persona set :personality "warm, brief"
thomas chat            # talk; it remembers
thomas remember "I'm vegetarian"

Name, pronouns, voice, personality. One file: ~/.thomas/thomas.sexpr. It remembers your recent turns and your notes between messages; thomas forget clears the conversation.

Snap!

One brain.

Claude, Codex or Gemini, through the Agent Client Protocol, or free models through OpenRouter (text only). Claude is the default; Codex and Gemini need autonomy on (see below). Thomas never ships a credential; you log in to the brain you chose yourself.

From your phone.

Text your assistant from anywhere. First make your own Telegram bot (about two minutes, on your phone): in Telegram, search for @BotFather (the blue check mark), send /newbot, give it any name and then a username that ends in bot. BotFather replies with a long token -- the bot's password, so keep it to yourself. Then, on your computer:

thomas telegram setup      # paste that token (it is not shown)
thomas telegram run        # send /pair CODE to it from your phone
thomas telegram install    # keep it answering in the background

Only the chat that sent the code reaches your assistant; other people's private chats are told it is private, and groups are ignored. It works only with autonomy off, and your computer must be on and logged in. Telegram bot chats are not end-to-end encrypted: your messages and the replies pass through Telegram's servers.

What it can see.

The plain terms. No fine print.

Thomas runs on your machine; your prompts go to the brain's provider (Anthropic, OpenAI or Google) under your own login, or, on the free brain, to OpenRouter and the company running the free model. The free brain only answers in text. With the default settings the only other brain Thomas runs is Claude, in an empty scratch folder, and Thomas refuses every request it is asked to approve: reading, writing or running anything. Claude may still run a few read-only commands such as whoami and pwd without asking, so what reaches the provider includes your prompt, your persona settings, your account name and a temporary folder path, along with what Claude itself sends (such as your platform and the date). So that it remembers you, each message also carries your recent turns and your notes, kept in two files under ~/.thomas that Thomas creates readable only by your user account; (memory :keep nil) stops keeping and sending them; files already written stay until you delete them.

Codex and Gemini read files outside that folder even in their asking modes, so Thomas runs them only after you turn autonomy on. Reading another app's stored key is off unless you turn it on.

On the free brain, your prompt, persona, notes and recent turns go to OpenRouter and to the outside company running the free model, which may keep, train on, or publish them.

What it may do.

Three switches in ~/.thomas/thomas.sexpr. All of them start off.

:autonomy Off
Off: the brain can answer but not act (Claude only; the free brain never acts either way). On: the Claude, Codex or Gemini brain may read, write and run commands anywhere your user account can, without asking, and Codex and Gemini become available.
:borrow-credentials Off
Off: the brain sees only your environment. On: it may also read a key another app stored.
:ledger Off
Off: Thomas keeps no ledger (Claude still saves its usual session transcript under ~/.claude/projects). On: each turn's event kinds and a short summary also go to a local redis stream, if one runs.
